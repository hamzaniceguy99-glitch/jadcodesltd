How we work

An honest position on accessibility

Three things we will not pretend. An audit reports the pages we tested on the day we tested them — content changes, plugins and embedded media introduce new barriers, so it is not a certification and nobody can issue one. Automated tools find only a minority of real problems, which is why we test with a keyboard and a screen reader. And the legal duty stays with you: public sector bodies must meet the relevant WCAG level so far as reasonably practicable and publish an accessibility statement under the 2018 Regulations, and private organisations have duties under the Equality Act — we can draft and advise technically, but we give no legal advice and cannot assume the obligation.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Can you certify our site as accessible?

No, and nobody can. There is no certification scheme behind WCAG. An audit tells you what failed on the pages tested on the day, which is useful and genuinely different from a guarantee.

We ran a scanner and scored well. Is that enough?

Usually not. Automated tools catch a minority of real barriers and cannot tell whether a form is completable with a keyboard or whether a screen reader announces anything useful. That part needs manual testing.

What about those accessibility overlay widgets?

We do not recommend them. They sit on top of the problem rather than fixing it, disabled users widely report that they make things worse, and they have featured in legal claims rather than preventing them.

Do we have to publish an accessibility statement?

Public sector bodies do, under the 2018 Regulations, and it must be accurate — a statement claiming full compliance on a site with known failures is worse than one listing them with a plan. We draft it; publishing it remains yours.

Do we own the code?

Yes, on payment, with full repository access and documentation. Ask any developer this before signing.

Will this protect us from a claim?

We give no legal advice and cannot promise that. Fixing real barriers and publishing an honest statement is the substantive thing you can do; the legal position is a question for your own advisers.
